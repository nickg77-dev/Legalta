# Rapid Experiment Designer

Turn a concept into the cheapest possible test that produces a real learning, then force an honest decision on the result.

## Core principle
The prototype is a question, not a product. Quick, dirty and cheap beats polished - a polished prototype blocks feedback because users assume it is finished. Every experiment must be able to fail.

## Process

### Step 1: Extract the riskiest assumption
Ask: "If one belief being wrong would kill this concept, which is it?" Write it as a falsifiable statement, e.g. "Parents of picky eaters will pay a monthly subscription for personalised seasonal meal guidance."

If desirability, feasibility and viability assumptions are tangled, test desirability first (it kills most concepts).

### Step 2: Design the experiment card
Produce this card, plain text, copy-paste ready:

EXPERIMENT CARD
- Assumption: [falsifiable statement]
- Test: [method, from menu below]
- Metric: [one number]
- Success threshold: [set BEFORE running, e.g. "3 of 10 interviewees pre-order at £4.99/month"]
- Cost: [£ and hours]
- Deadline: [date]
- Kill criterion: [result that means stop]

### Step 3: Pick the cheapest adequate method

| Method | Tests | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storyboard walkthrough | Does the concept make sense in the user's life | £0 | Half day |
| Role playing | Service interactions, conversations | £0 | Half day |
| Scale model / paper mock-up | Physical or layout concepts | Under £20 | 1 day |
| Landing page + small ad spend | Demand, message, price | £50-150 | 2-4 days |
| Concierge (deliver the service manually) | Willingness to pay, real usage | Time only | 1-2 weeks |
| Wizard of Oz (fake the backend) | Product behaviour, retention | Low | 1-2 weeks |
| Pre-order / deposit ask | Real purchase intent | £0 | Within interviews |

Default bias: the method one row cheaper than Nick's instinct.

### Step 4: Force the decision
When results come in, compare against the pre-set threshold and issue one verdict:
- PERSEVERE: threshold met - name the next riskiest assumption
- PIVOT: signal exists but concept needs a structural change - propose 2 pivot options
- KILL: below kill criterion - say so directly, list what was learned and what it cost to learn

No verdict may be softened because effort was invested. Sunk cost is not evidence.

## Example (abridged)
Concept: seasonal produce guidance for parents of picky eaters.
Riskiest assumption: parents actively seek help with picky eating and will give an email address for a free seasonal "one new veg a week" plan.
Test: one-page landing site, £75 of targeted ads.
Metric: email signup rate. Success: 8% or above. Kill: under 2%.
Cost: £75 + 6 hours. Deadline: 7 days.

## Never do
- Never design an experiment without a numeric success threshold agreed before it runs
- Never let a prototype take more than 2 weeks or £200 without an explicit justification
- Never test more than one riskiest assumption per experiment
- Never interpret compliments, page views or social likes as validation - only behaviour and commitments count
- Never recommend building the real product while a cheaper adequate test exists
