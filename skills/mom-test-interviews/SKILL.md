# Mom Test Interviews

Turn fluffy validation conversations into hard evidence about customer problems, using Rob Fitzpatrick's The Mom Test.

## The three rules
1. Talk about their life, not your idea
2. Ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future
3. Talk less, listen more

## Mode A: Design an interview script

Ask first: What is the riskiest assumption being tested? Who exactly is the interviewee segment? (If Nick cannot name the riskiest assumption, help him write it as a falsifiable statement before drafting questions.)

Then produce a script with:
- 1 opening framing line (casual, no pitch)
- 5-7 questions, all past-tense and specific
- 2 digging follow-ups ("Talk me through the last time that happened", "What did you do about it?")
- 1 commitment ask appropriate to the stage (time, reputation or money)

Question quality bar:
- Bad: "Would you buy an app that plans seasonal meals?" (hypothetical, invites lying)
- Good: "Tell me about the last time you planned the week's meals. What did you use? What was annoying?"
- Bad: "Do you think picky eating is a big problem?"
- Good: "When did your child last refuse a meal? What did you do next? What have you tried or paid for to fix this?"

## Mode B: Analyse a conversation

When Nick shares notes or a transcript, classify every notable statement:
- FACT: specific past behaviour or verifiable detail (strong evidence)
- COMMITMENT: gave up time, reputation or money (strongest evidence)
- COMPLIMENT: "sounds great", "I'd definitely use that" (zero evidence - deflect and dig next time)
- FLUFF: generic claims, hypotheticals, future promises (zero evidence)

Then output:
- Evidence table (statement, classification, what it proves or fails to prove)
- Verdict: does this conversation support or weaken the riskiest assumption?
- The 3 questions Nick should have asked and should ask next time

## Commitment ladder
Advancement means the customer gave up something:
1. Time: agreed a scheduled follow-up or working session
2. Reputation: intro to their boss, team or peers; public endorsement
3. Money: letter of intent, pre-order, deposit, paid pilot

"They said they loved it" with no commitment = the meeting failed. Say so plainly.

## Output format
Plain text, copy-paste ready for Google Docs. Interview scripts numbered. Analysis as a simple table (columns: Statement | Type | Implication).

## Never do
- Never include a pitch or idea description in a discovery script
- Never write hypothetical or future-tense questions ("would you", "could you see yourself")
- Never count compliments as validation, even if Nick is excited about them
- Never let a session end without defining the next riskiest assumption to test
- Never soften a failed-meeting verdict to be agreeable
