# Cold Outreach - Snail Mail

A cold letter has to do everything a cold email does with none of its safety nets. No
inbox to re-open, no click to measure, no second touch three days later if the first one
lands wrong. That constraint should drive every decision below, not just the copy.

---

## Step 1: Decide whether a hook is load-bearing

Cold-email doctrine treats the personalised opener as mandatory - no opener, no send.
Physical mail doesn't have that luxury: the researched fact often doesn't exist for a
given recipient, and holding a lead back for want of one wastes a real, costed letter.

- Design the template so the hook is a genuinely optional first line, not a placeholder
  that leaves an obvious gap when empty. If the letter reads awkwardly with the hook
  removed, the surrounding paragraphs are leaning on it - fix the surrounding paragraphs,
  not the hook.
- Apply the cold-email personalisation-bridge test anyway, even though the hook is
  optional: when one exists, does it connect to the reason you're writing, or does it
  just prove you did research? "As you recently committed €20 million to renew your
  fleet, a number of those vehicles may be eligible" bridges. "As you recently committed
  €20 million to renew your fleet, we wanted to reach out" doesn't - delete and rewrite.
- Never let "no hook" become "no letter." That's a targeting decision (does this lead
  meet the ICP), not a copy decision.

## Step 2: Build the hook from a strict fact hierarchy

When a hook does exist, don't just report the strongest available research note verbatim
- rewrite it to one plain sentence, in priority order:

1. **A real fleet-size or volume number**, if one is confirmed. Lead with it.
2. **Duration or generational history**, if no number exists but the business's age or
   family continuity is well sourced.
3. **A recent, dated, quantified event** (a renewal programme, a tender, a leadership
   change) if neither of the above exists.
4. **Nothing.** Leave it blank rather than force a sentence the research doesn't support.
   A negative or empty research note ("no fleet detail found") is a signal to skip, not a
   prompt to invent.

House pattern: **"As [subject], [consequence]."** One sentence. Vary the subject between
second person ("As you operate...") for current facts the reader can confirm about
themselves, and third person by name ("As [Company] has been through three generations
of family ownership...") for historical facts about the entity itself, then fold back to
"your" once you reach anything the reader currently owns or controls: "...some of your
fleet of 70 vehicles are likely to be eligible." Read each case on its own rather than
forcing one register throughout - a sole trader reads more naturally as "you" throughout,
a multi-generation company almost always needs the mixed register to sound like a person
wrote it rather than a script.

Never use the word "confirmed" in the hook itself. It's true of your research process,
not something the reader needs told to them.

## Step 3: One letterhead block, not two

Physical letters carry real postal conventions email doesn't: a return address, a
recipient address positioned for a windowed envelope, a company registration footer.
Getting this wrong doesn't bounce silently, it prints wrong or misses the postal window
entirely.

- **One full identity block**, not a full block plus a separately repeated compact
  version of the same address. If you're representing both a "full letterhead" and a
  "return address visible through the envelope window" convention, keep them functionally
  distinct or drop the second one - don't stack the same text twice with no reason
  visible to the reader. The window-visible return line is a print-production detail that
  belongs at the postal-service formatting stage, not in a content draft.
- One block should carry address, phone, email, and website together, not split across
  the header and buried again in the body.
- Recipient block: full legal name, street address, postal code and city, and the
  destination country as its own explicit final line if it differs from the sender's own
  default country (many bulk-post services silently reject international mail without
  it).
- Subject/Oggetto line names the recipient, not just the topic: "Compensation for
  [Entity]'s trucks..." reads as addressed to them; "Compensation for trucks..." reads as
  a mail-merge artifact.
- Salutation: named recipient gets their name and honorific. No named contact gets a
  role-based address ("Alla cortese attenzione della Direzione" or the local-language
  equivalent), never a placeholder that reads as broken personalisation.

## Step 4: Cut the legal or technical scaffolding to what a reader actually needs

Cold email gets away with light claims because a reply is cheap to send either way. A
physical letter asserting a legal or financial entitlement needs to survive a sceptical
read, but that doesn't mean citing everything.

- State the institutional facts (who ruled, when, what they found) in plain sentences.
  Drop case citation numbers from the persuasive prose entirely, or cite them succinctly
  in parentheses once each, only if the reader explicitly needs to trace and verify them
  later - a bare docket number embedded mid-sentence serves the writer's sense of rigour,
  not the reader's understanding.
- One eligibility test, stated as a short list or a tight paragraph, not scattered across
  multiple paragraphs with the criteria repeated in different words each time.
- State the no-cost or no-risk proposition once, clearly. If you're echoing it a second
  time for deliberate emphasis (a proven pattern in cold direct mail), do it once more at
  most and make sure it's a deliberate choice, not something that crept in from merging
  two drafts.
- Name the institution precisely on first mention ("the Court of Justice of the European
  Union") if the letter uses "the Court" again later - a bare "the Court" two paragraphs
  after several different courts have been named is genuinely ambiguous to the reader,
  not just imprecise.

## Step 5: Run the humanise-writing strip pass, then the letter-specific checks

Read `humanise-writing`'s Step 2 (strip pass) and Step 3 (five-lens judgement) in full and
apply them here in **formal mode** - a cold letter asserting a legal or financial claim is
closer to a client proposal than a conversational email, so personality (that skill's
Step 4) stays off. On top of that skill's checks, for a letter specifically:

- **Does it read fine with the hook removed?** If not, the surrounding paragraphs are
  under-written on their own. Fix them, don't add to the hook.
- **Quality-gate it before it goes to print**, the same way a good cold-email skill scores
  a draft before sending: does this open with the reader's situation, not yours? Does it
  ask for one small thing, not several? Would the "no cost" and "no risk" claims survive
  the recipient's own lawyer or accountant reading them literally?
- **Read it as the person who has to physically discard it.** A letter that can be
  skimmed and binned in four seconds has failed regardless of how accurate its legal
  content is.

## Step 6: Compliance guardrails specific to claims and entitlements

- Never state or imply a guaranteed outcome. A hedged range or ceiling ("up to €X") is
  fine when it's an approved, already-public figure (on the client's own website, in
  their own prior materials) - confirm this explicitly before using a specific number,
  don't assume a competitor's or a template's number is safe to reuse just because it
  reads well.
- Every factual claim in the letter should trace to something in a source register or a
  named, dated primary source, the same discipline good cold-email skills apply to a
  claimed statistic or case study.
- If the letter claims an existing client relationship or case study, confirm the actual
  legal and factual relationship before naming it. A materially stronger version of a
  proof point ("we represent the City of Zurich") is worth holding back until the
  underlying relationship is confirmed, not used because it reads better.

## Step 7: Physical-mail production mechanics

These don't apply to email at all, and skipping them means letters that print wrong or
get rejected by the bulk-post service, not letters that quietly under-perform.

- Confirm the bulk-post provider's address-window position (left or right) and layout
  requirements (address area coordinates, postage area, restricted margins) before
  finalising any letterhead design - these are usually published in millimetres from
  each edge and are non-negotiable for automated printing.
- Confirm the destination country's own postal address format (line order, postal code
  placement, whether the country name is required on every letter or can be omitted for
  the sender's default country).
- Confirm whether the provider wants one PDF per letter or one continuous PDF containing
  all letters back to back - most bulk-post mail-merge services support both, but pick
  one and be consistent across the batch.
- For the underlying mail-merge data: keep exactly one address and one named contact per
  row. Where a source has more than one office or more than one named contact for the
  same entity, split it into separate rows and link them with a note, rather than trying
  to represent two addressees in one merge row.

## Step 8: Before delivering a batch

- Sample-check a handful of populated letters, not just the blank template, including at
  least one with no hook and one with the longest available hook.
- If hooks were generated at scale from unstructured research notes rather than written
  individually, say so plainly and flag which ones are template-shaped as a result, since
  volume generation reintroduces exactly the templated-feeling problem hand-written hooks
  were built to avoid.
- Flag, don't silently resolve, any row where the underlying data is ambiguous - multiple
  possible contacts, a founder who may no longer be the right person to address, an
  address that fails standard postal validation. A wrong guess sent by post is far more
  visible and harder to walk back than a bounced email.
